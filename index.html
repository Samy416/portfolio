Side Project: An AI Content Engine

Outside of work, I design and build automated systems that turn a single idea into a finished, published video — with almost no manual production work in between. It started as a way to learn real software engineering by building something with enough moving parts to actually break, and turned into a full pipeline I maintain and keep extending.

1

Research & Idea

Topic and angle generation, checked for originality and accuracy.

2

Script

An LLM drafts, self-reviews, and refines narration for pacing and clarity.

3

Voice & Visuals

AI voice synthesis and AI-generated imagery, matched to the script beat by beat.

4

Assembly

Automated video composition with captions and motion via ffmpeg.

5

Human Review

Nothing publishes until a person signs off on accuracy and quality.

6

Publish & Learn

Performance feedback flows back into the next round of ideas.

6 content channels running on one engine
60+ videos produced end-to-end
100% human-reviewed before publishing

One engine, many channels

Each content channel is a configuration profile — voice, visual style, and review rules — not a separate codebase. The same pipeline runs every channel; what makes each one distinct is data, not code.

Built responsibly

A human review gate blocks publishing on anything that hasn't been checked for accuracy and quality — the automation speeds up production, it doesn't remove the judgment call.

Stack

Python orchestration, an LLM for writing and reasoning, text-to-speech and image-generation APIs, ffmpeg for video composition, and a small dashboard for tracking runs and cost.
